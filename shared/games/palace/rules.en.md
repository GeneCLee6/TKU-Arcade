## What is Palace Bluff?

A bluffing game at an imperial court. Everyone has two secret backers at court and a little silver. On your turn you take one action, and many actions need a certain role: you may claim any role you like, whether you hold it or not. Anyone who thinks you are lying can challenge you, and whoever is wrong loses a backer. Lose both and you are out; the last one standing wins. You argue on your own voice call or face to face; the site deals the cards, runs the clock and keeps the log.

It plays like the card game Coup (Rikki Tahta, Indie Boards & Cards); the name, the court, the roles, the wording and the look here are our own. Palace Bluff is a working title.

## The cards

- The deck has five roles, three of each: **Treasurer**, **Imperial Guard**, **Chief Eunuch**, **Strategist** and **Empress Dowager**.
- Everyone starts with 2 face-down backers and 2 silver. With 2 players, whoever goes first starts with 1 silver.
- Your backers are secret. When you lose one it is turned face up for everyone to see and stays that way.

## Your turn

Pick one action. Anyone may take any of them; the role an action names is only a claim.

- **Stipend**: +1 silver. Nobody can stop it.
- **Royal grant**: +2 silver. Anyone may block it by claiming the Treasurer.
- **Storm the palace**: pay 7 silver, and the target loses a backer. Nobody can stop it. With 10 silver or more it is the only action you may take.
- **Levy** (claims the Treasurer): +3 silver.
- **Arrest** (claims the Imperial Guard): pay 3 silver, and the target loses a backer. The target may block it by claiming the Empress Dowager.
- **Extort** (claims the Chief Eunuch): take 2 silver from the target (or the 1 they have). The target may block it by claiming the Chief Eunuch or the Strategist.
- **Scheme** (claims the Strategist): draw 2 cards from the deck, then keep as many as you had face down and shuffle the rest back.

## Challenges

1. When you claim a role, everyone else gets a window to **challenge** you, or to pass.
2. If you do not hold the role, you were bluffing: you lose a backer and your action does not happen. Silver paid for an arrest comes back.
3. If you do hold it, you show it: it is shuffled back into the deck, you draw a new card, and the challenger loses a backer. Your action goes ahead.
4. Blocks are claims too. After a block, everyone else (the actor included) gets a window to challenge the blocker, in the same way. A block that stands stops the action; silver paid for a blocked arrest is gone.
5. Only one challenge or block counts each time: the first one in closes the window.

## The clock

Every window is open to everyone who may answer at once, with a countdown. When it runs out, everyone who has not answered passes. Choosing which backer to lose, or which cards to keep after a scheme, gets twice as long; when time runs out the first card is lost, or you keep the cards you had.

## Options

Set by the host in the waiting room.

- **Time to respond**: 10, 15 or 20 seconds, 15 by default.

## When someone is away

The host can skip an absent player whenever the game waits on them: on their turn they take a stipend (or storm the next player, with 10 silver or more), in a window they pass, and when they must choose a card the first one is used.

## The log

Every claim, challenge, block and lost backer is written in the log, newest first, so you can argue about it on your call.

## Players

2 to 6 players. A game takes about 15 minutes.
