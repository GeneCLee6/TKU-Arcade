## What is the full edition?

The full edition plays closer to the traditional game: four dice read as combinations, a roll for your family background, the imperial examinations, silver changing hands in office, choices to make on the way, and in the end retirement or exile. Whoever ends with the most silver wins.

The board is **our own compilation from public-domain knowledge of the Qing civil service**, 236 offices in 53 departments, with event zones such as the capital evaluation. The titles and grades are historical, but which office leads where is game design, not history, and many details are our reconstruction of how the traditional game plays. We do not claim it is the "authentic" version.

## Goal

Everyone starts with 100 taels of silver, and the common pot holds 100 taels for each player. **When only one player is left in office, the game ends.** After rank comparison and the share-out of the pot, **the player with the most silver wins**.

## Origin

Your first roll does not move you. It decides your family background instead:

| You roll | You start as | Chance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Four of a kind, a straight, or two pairs with a virtue pair | Yinsheng (Hereditary Student) | 8.3% |
| A pair or three of virtue | Shengyuan (County Scholar) | 10.8% |
| A pair or three of talent | Jiansheng (Purchased Studentship), paying 10 taels to the pot | 10.8% |
| A pair or three of merit or good conduct, or two plain pairs | Tongsheng (Exam Candidate) | 26.2% |
| Anything else | Baiding (Commoner) | 43.8% |

A player whose roll shows **both a 4 and a 6** belongs to a **Banner** household; everyone else is **Civilian** (a 23.3% chance). This illustrates a Qing institution, the divide between Banner people and civilians: Banner players can take the road through the Banner companies, the Banner clerks, the Banner command and the Imperial Household Department, and can join the Imperial Guard; a civilian needs a military jinshi degree to become a guard.

Once you have left your origin, nothing ever sends you back to one.

## On your turn

1. Press **Roll** and the server rolls four six-sided dice for you.
2. Each die is a result: 4 virtue, 6 talent, 5 merit, 3 good conduct, 2 mediocre, 1 graft, from best to worst.
3. Move by the combination.
4. **Every turn has a result.** If the roll makes no combination (or virtue cancels graft), roll again, until one does; the turn passes only once you have moved.

## Combinations

| Combination | Moves | Chance per roll |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A pair | One step by that result | 51.9% |
| Three of a kind | Two steps by that result | 9.0% |
| Two plain pairs (no virtue pair) | One step for each pair, the better first | 4.6% |
| Two pairs with a virtue pair | Two virtue steps | 1.9% |
| A straight (four pips in a row) | One virtue step, and the pot pays a reward of 10 taels | 5.6% |
| Four of a kind | A peerage from junior third grade up (the next peerage for a peer; the palace examination's top place for a palace candidate, and the military one's for a military jinshi); three virtue steps for anyone else | 0.4% |
| Four grafts (four 1s) | Dismissal (not from an origin, where there is no post to lose) | 0.1% |
| Two or more virtues with a graft | The graft is forgiven, and the roll is void: roll again | 4.5% |
| No combination | Roll again | 22.2% |

Every cell of the board says where each of the six results leads from it. Roughly: virtue goes up two steps, talent and merit one, good conduct moves you to another post of the same grade, mediocre keeps you where you are or sends you to an evaluation (in the senior first grade or a peerage it means retiring with honours, and for officers of the army, the Banners and the Guard in the second grade or the junior first, retiring), and graft demotes you. Some tables play a variant where virtue and graft in one roll simply cancel out; we keep the rule above.

## Choices

- **Offered posts**: a copyist's post at one of the compilation offices (謄錄), a teacher at a Banner school (教習) or a special examination by summons (召試), marked on the board. When the dice would send you there you may **accept**, and take the post and its route (the copyists, after their service, are commended into a low regular post), or **decline**, and stay where you were; you roll as usual next turn.
- **Buying office**: when the dice would make you an astronomy student, a court physician's assistant or another minor technical post (marked on the board), and you hold at least a licentiate's or a purchased studentship's standing, you may instead **buy a regular office**: 5 taels to the pot for each rank step above the minor post. The capital's offices go up to a director (senior fifth grade), the provinces' to an intendant (senior fourth grade), and a capital official buying a provincial post pays 5 taels less (5 at least). Only what you can pay for is offered. A bought office is the **irregular route**: education posts, censors and the commissions that need the regular route refuse it. The host may turn buying off.
- Every choice has a countdown (30 seconds unless the host sets another). If nobody answers, the post is declined, or the minor post taken. A player who is gone is answered for the same way.

## Events and dismissal

- **Evaluations and events**: mediocre in many posts sends you to the capital evaluation (京察), the provincial evaluation (大計), the Hanlin's grand examination (大考) or military merit (軍功); graft in the highest civil posts to a disciplinary review (處分); good conduct in the palace titles to imperial favour (曠典). There you roll once more at once, and its result decides from the post you held: up a step or two, back to the post, down, or dismissed, sometimes with silver from the pot or an honorary title. With no combination you roll again until there is a result.
- **Dismissal**: a dismissed official waits for restoration. On each later turn a virtue result restores the post held, talent the next post down (at most a grade lower), and three or four grafts exile you to the frontier (**Changshu**), out of the game; anything else keeps you waiting.

## Silver

- **Courtesy call**: when you land in a department where someone else already serves at a different grade, the junior of you two pays the senior 5 taels.
- **Purchases and fines**: a purchased studentship costs 10 taels, a purchased office 20, and dismissal a fine of 10, all paid to the pot.
- **Commissions**: an examiner, an education commissioner, a salt or ward censor, a granary superintendent and the inner court's duties earn a fee from the pot when taken; rolling graft while holding one costs a fine of 10 taels, or 15 for examiners and education commissioners.
- **Rewards and side posts**: jieyuan, huiyuan, bangyan, tanhua, the top military graduate and a few others are rewarded, and the salt controller, the river and grain transport directors and the Grand Council pay a side income, all from the pot (nothing, once the pot is empty).
- **Congratulations**: when someone retires, everyone still in office sends a gift: 5 taels for retiring, 10 for retiring with honours. For the grand congratulations everyone pays 30 taels, the exiled too, but not the retired or anyone waiting in dismissal.
- **Honorary titles**: a governor-general or a governor holds a concurrent title, and imperial favour may give one; up to two, each counts a grade more in the rank comparison.
- Silver can go below zero as a debt, which is settled at the end like any other balance.

## Retirement and exile

Reaching **Zhishi** (Retired), **Yugao Ronggui** (Retired with Honours) or **Dahe** (Grand Congratulations) retires you, and you roll no more. The first two keep the grade you held; the grand congratulations is the top of the board. The exiled are out of the game and are compared at the end as if holding no rank. The host may have the game end at the first grand congratulations.

## Settlement

1. **Rank comparison**: between every two players, the junior pays the senior 5 taels for each grade between them (senior and junior grades count alike; a peerage is one step above the first grade, and the grand congratulations one more; each honorary title adds one, never past a peerage). The ladder of grades (品級考) on the board shows where everyone stands.
2. **Share-out**: the pot is split evenly among all players, and what does not divide goes to the poorest.
3. The most silver wins. On equal silver the higher rank wins, and players equal on both share a place.

There are at most 100 rounds. If the game has not ended by then, everyone is compared as they stand and the pot is shared.

Once the game is over, the **career routes** under the result show every player's turns from the birth roll to the end: what the dice made, where it led, what was answered and the silver.

## Players

2 to 6 players. A four-player game takes about 20 minutes, a six-player game about 35.
