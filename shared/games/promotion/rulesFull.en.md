## What is the full edition?

The full edition plays closer to the traditional game: four dice read as combinations, a roll for your family background, the imperial examinations, silver changing hands in office, and in the end retirement or exile. Whoever ends with the most silver wins.

The board is **our own compilation from public-domain knowledge of the Qing civil service**, about 180 offices. The titles and grades are historical, but which office leads where is game design, not history, and many details are our reconstruction of how the traditional game plays. We do not claim it is the "authentic" version.

## Goal

Everyone starts with 100 taels of silver, and the common pot holds 100 taels for each player. **When only one player is left in office, the game ends.** After rank comparison and the share-out of the pot, **the player with the most silver wins**.

## Origin

Your first roll does not move you. It decides your family background instead:

| You roll | You start as | Chance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Four of a kind, a straight, or two pairs with a virtue pair | Yinsheng (Hereditary Student) | 8.3% |
| A pair or three of virtue | Shengyuan (County Scholar) | 10.8% |
| A pair or three of talent | Jiansheng (Purchased Studentship), paying 10 taels to the pot | 10.8% |
| A pair or three of merit or good conduct, or two plain pairs | Tongsheng (Exam Candidate) | 26.2% |
| Anything else | Baiding (Commoner) | 43.8% |

A player whose roll shows **both a 4 and a 6** belongs to a **Banner** household; everyone else is **Civilian** (a 23.3% chance). This illustrates a Qing institution, the divide between Banner people and civilians: Banner players can take the road through the Banner companies, the Banner clerks and the Banner command, and can join the Imperial Guard; a civilian needs a military jinshi degree to become a guard.

## On your turn

1. Press **Roll** and the server rolls four six-sided dice for you.
2. Each die is a result: 4 virtue, 6 talent, 5 merit, 3 good conduct, 2 mediocre, 1 graft, from best to worst.
3. Move by the combination. With no combination you roll again, up to 3 rolls a turn; if the third still has none, you stay where you are.

## Combinations

| Combination | Moves | Chance per roll |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A pair | One step by that result | 51.9% |
| Three of a kind | Two steps by that result | 9.0% |
| Two plain pairs (no virtue pair) | One step for each pair, the better first | 4.6% |
| Two pairs with a virtue pair | Two virtue steps | 1.9% |
| A straight (four pips in a row) | One virtue step, and the pot pays a reward of 10 taels | 5.6% |
| Four of a kind | A peerage from junior third grade up (the next peerage for a peer; the palace examination's top place for a palace candidate, and the military one's for a military jinshi); three virtue steps for anyone else | 0.4% |
| Four grafts (four 1s) | Dismissal | 0.1% |
| Two or more virtues with a graft | The graft is forgiven: the roll does not count and you roll again | 4.5% |
| No combination | Roll again | 22.2% |

Every cell of the board says where each of the six results leads from it. Roughly: virtue goes up two steps, talent and merit one, good conduct moves you to another post of the same grade or keeps you where you are, mediocre keeps you where you are (in the senior first grade or a peerage it means retiring with honours, and for officers of the army, the Banners and the Guard in the second grade or the junior first, retiring), and graft demotes you.

## The examinations

A county scholar sits the provincial examination: a pass makes a juren (the top pass is the jieyuan), a failure sends them back, and a middling result makes a fubang. A juren sits the metropolitan examination: a pass makes a palace candidate (the top pass is the huiyuan); a juren who does poorly may be picked by the selection of graduates and sent straight to a county or a teaching post. The palace examination failed nobody: it ranks a candidate as zhuangyuan, bangyan, tanhua, or a jinshi of the second or third class, who is then given a first post.

Degrees stay with you. A tribute student (bagong, suigong or fubang) is on the regular route, and above that come juren and jinshi. Some offices need a degree: education officials and censors need the regular route, the assistant grand secretary needs juren, and the Hanlin Academy and the grand secretaries need jinshi. Without it you land on the other office that cell names instead.

## Silver

- **Courtesy call**: when you land in a department where someone else already serves at a different grade, the junior of you two pays the senior 5 taels.
- **Purchases and fines**: a purchased studentship costs 10 taels, a purchased office 20, and dismissal a fine of 10, all paid to the pot.
- **Rewards and side posts**: jieyuan, huiyuan, bangyan, tanhua, the top military graduate and a few others are rewarded, and the education commissioner, the salt controller, the river and grain transport directors and the Grand Council pay a side income, all from the pot (nothing, once the pot is empty).
- **Congratulations**: when someone retires, everyone still in office sends a gift: 5 taels for retiring, 10 for retiring with honours, 20 for the grand congratulations.
- Silver can go below zero as a debt, which is settled at the end like any other balance.

## Retirement and exile

Reaching **Zhishi** (Retired), **Yugao Ronggui** (Retired with Honours) or **Dahe** (Grand Congratulations) retires you, and you roll no more. The first two keep the grade you held; the grand congratulations is the top of the board. A dismissed official who rolls graft again is exiled to the frontier (**Changshu**) and is out of the game, compared at the end as if holding no rank.

## Settlement

1. **Rank comparison**: between every two players, the junior pays the senior 5 taels for each grade between them (senior and junior grades count alike; a peerage is one step above the first grade, and the grand congratulations one more).
2. **Share-out**: the pot is split evenly among all players, and what does not divide goes to the poorest.
3. The most silver wins. On equal silver the higher rank wins, and players equal on both share a place.

There are at most 100 rounds. If the game has not ended by then, everyone is compared as they stand and the pot is shared.

## Players

2 to 6 players. A four-player game takes about half an hour, a six-player game about 45 minutes.
