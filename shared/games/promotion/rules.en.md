## What is Promotion?

Promotion is our take on *shengguan tu*, a traditional Chinese board game in which players race up the ranks of the imperial civil service. Its exact origins and early forms are still debated, so we make no claim to be the authentic version.

The board here is **a simplified Qing ladder written for this game**. The titles and grades are drawn from history, but which post leads where is game design, not history.

## The goal

Everyone starts as a commoner. **The first to reach Ronggui (Retired in Glory), at the centre of the board, wins at once.** If nobody gets there before the round limit, the game ends and the most senior office wins. Equal seniority shares a place.

## Your turn

1. Press **Roll**.
2. The result is Virtue, Talent, Merit or Graft.
3. Your piece moves to the office your current cell names for that result.
4. One roll a turn, then the next player. Every roll moves you, except graft on the commoner's cell, where you stay put (and the turn still passes).

There is nothing to decide: the fun is in the swings, and in watching your friends fall.

## The four results

- **Virtue**: the best result, going at least as high as Talent.
- **Talent**: going at least as high as Merit.
- **Merit**: a step up.
- **Graft**: you are caught, and demoted. A commoner has nowhere to fall and stays put.

Pick any cell on the board to see where each result leads from it.

## Options

The host picks these in the waiting room.

- **Dice**: a four-sided top, where each result is equally likely, or four six-sided dice.
- **Round limit**: from 10 to 200 rounds, 60 by default. A round is everyone rolling once.

With four six-sided dice, count how many show a red face. The 1 and the 4 are red, as on Chinese dice. The table is our own, not a historical one.

| Red faces | Result | Chance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3 or 4 | Virtue | 11.1% |
| 2 | Talent | 29.6% |
| 1 | Merit | 39.5% |
| 0 | Graft | 19.8% |

## Players

Two to six players. Games with more players run longer. If someone drops out, the host can skip their turns or roll for them, since no turn holds a decision.
