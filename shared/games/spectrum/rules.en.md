## What is Spectrum?

A party game about how differently people measure the same thing. Each round one player guesses and everyone else gets a secret number from 1 to 10. A topic with two ends comes up, such as "late-night snacks": 1 is the one you least want, 10 the one you most want. Each answerer says where their number sits with one answer, and the guesser works out whose number is the largest and whose is the smallest.

The idea of saying a secret number through an answer comes from the card game ito (Arclight); the topics, wording, look and scoring here are our own.

## One round

1. A topic is drawn, with what 1 means and what 10 means.
2. Everyone but the guesser gets a secret number from 1 to 10 that only they can see. **No two answerers hold the same number**, unless the host allows repeats.
3. Each answerer writes **one answer**: one thing or one word that sits where their number sits between the two ends. You can change it until everyone has answered.
4. Once every answer is in, they are all shown together. Until then you only see who has answered, not what.
5. The guesser picks the answer they think holds **the largest number** and the one with **the smallest**. They must be two different answers.
6. The numbers are turned over on a line from 1 to 10, and the round is scored.
7. The guesser starts the next round, and the role moves one seat on.

## Answers

- **No numbers.** Digits of any kind are refused, and so are signs such as ① or ½. Chinese number words are allowed, because so many ordinary words contain them, but do not use them to give your number away.
- One thing or one word, up to 30 characters.
- Chat is closed while the answers are written and while the guesser guesses. It opens again at the reveal.

## Scoring

- **Every right pick scores 1 point for the guesser and 1 point for the answerer who was picked.** A guesser can win up to 2 points in a round.
- If numbers can repeat and two answers share the largest (or the smallest) number, either one counts as right.
- After everyone has guessed once, the highest total wins. Equal totals share the place.

## Time

With the clock on, answerers have 90 seconds and the guesser has 60.

- An answerer who has not answered when the time runs out sits the round out: their answer is not in the guess and they cannot score, but their number is still shown at the reveal.
- If fewer than two answers came in, there is nothing to compare: the numbers are shown and nobody scores.
- If the guesser runs out of time, the round scores nothing.

## Options

The host picks these in the waiting room.

- **The guesser picks**: the largest and the smallest (the default), or everyone in order. In order, the guesser puts every answer from the smallest number to the largest, and each answer in its right place scores 1 point for the guesser and 1 point for that answerer. Equal numbers may go in either order.
- **Numbers can repeat**: off by default. Turned on, each answerer draws their own number, so two may hold the same one.
- **Everyone guesses twice**: off by default. Turned on, the game is twice as long.
- **90 s to answer, 60 s to guess**: on by default. Turned off, there is no clock.

## Players

4 to 11 players: one guesser and 3 to 10 answerers. If an answerer drops out, the host can skip them for the round. If the guesser drops out, the host can skip their guess, or start the next round for them.
