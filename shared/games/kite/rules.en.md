## What is Kite Day?

A co-operative card game for a windy afternoon on the Tamsui riverside. The whole table works together to send up five kite trains, many kites on one line, each colour in order from 1 to 5. The catch: you hold your kites facing out. You see everyone's hand but your own, and the only way to tell a friend what they hold is to pass them a note.

It plays like the card game Hanabi (Antoine Bauza); the name, the kite theme, the wording and the look here are our own. Kite Day is a working title.

## The kites

- Five colours: **Red**, **Gold**, **Jade**, **Blue** and **White**. Each colour has ten kites: three 1s, two each of 2, 3 and 4, and one 5.
- Each colour has a mark too (circle, diamond, triangle, square, cross), so the colours can be told apart without seeing colour.
- Each player holds 5 kites with 2 or 3 players, 4 kites with 4 or 5 players. The rest is the deck.
- The table starts with **8 notes** and no snapped lines.

## Your turn

Do exactly one of these three things.

1. **Pass a note** (costs 1 note): pick a friend, then a colour or a number. The note points at **all** of their kites of that colour, or of that number, and tells them that the others are not. It must point at at least one kite. With no notes left you cannot pass one.
2. **Set a kite aside** (gets 1 note back): pick one of your own kites; it goes onto the discards for everyone to see, and you draw a new one. You cannot set one aside while all 8 notes are in hand.
3. **Fly a kite**: pick one of your own kites and send it up. If it is the next number on its colour's train (a 1 on an empty train, a 2 after a 1 and so on) it goes up. If not, its line **snaps**: it goes onto the discards and the table loses one of its 3 chances. Flying a 5 finishes that train and gets a note back.

After setting aside or flying you draw a new kite, if the deck has any. A new kite goes to the front of your hand, so card 1 is always your newest.

On a phone a note takes two taps (a friend, then a colour or number), and so does your own kite (the kite, then Fly it or Set aside).

## What you know

You cannot see your own kites, but each one shows what the notes have told you: a known colour paints it, a known number labels it, and under it the numbers and colours that are ruled out are struck through. This is only a memory aid: it is worked out from the notes and nothing else. Your friends see the same marks under your kites, so they know what you know.

The log lists every note, every kite flown, snapped and set aside; the discards show every kite that is gone, by colour.

## Talking

The notes are the only way to tell anyone about their own kites. On your call, chat away about anything else, but do not say what someone holds or hint at it another way. Chat is closed until the day ends.

## The end

- **The third snapped line** ends the day at once.
- **Every train full** (all 5s flying) ends it at the top score.
- **The deck runs out**: once the last kite is drawn, everyone, the one who drew it too, has one more turn, and then the day ends.

The score is the kites flying: the length of every train added up, 25 at most (30 with the rainbow). Everyone shares it.

## Options

Set by the host in the waiting room.

- **Rainbow kites (a sixth colour)**: ten more kites in a sixth colour, the rainbow. A note cannot name the rainbow; instead **every colour note points at rainbow kites too**. A kite pointed at by two different colours must be a rainbow one. Off by default.
- **Seconds per turn (0 = off)**: 0 to 300, 0 by default. When the clock runs out the turn passes with nothing done.

Spectators never see anyone's kites, so that nobody can peek at their own hand from a second window.

## When someone is away

If the player whose turn it is is away, the host can skip them: their turn passes with nothing done.

## Players

2 to 5 players. A game takes about 30 minutes.
