## What is Texas Hold'em?

The best-known poker game, no-limit, for play chips only. Everyone gets two cards of their own, five cards are dealt face up in the middle for everyone to share, and you make the best five-card hand out of the seven. Bet when you think you are ahead, or to make others think so; fold when you are not. The site deals, keeps the pot and the side pots, and reads every hand at the end.

The chips are play chips: they are worth nothing, nobody pays to sit down, and everyone starts again with the same stack in every game.

## A hand

- One player has the **dealer button**. The player to the left of the button posts the **small blind** and the next one the **big blind**; with two players the button posts the small blind.
- Everyone is dealt two cards face down. Betting goes round from the player left of the big blind (with two players, from the button).
- Then come the **flop** (three cards face up), the **turn** (a fourth) and the **river** (a fifth), each followed by a round of betting that starts from the first player still in left of the button.
- The button moves one seat to the left after every hand, past anyone who is out of chips.

## Betting

- On your turn you may **fold**, **check** (when nobody has bet), **call** (match the bet), **bet** or **raise**.
- No limit: you may bet everything you have at any time. The smallest bet is the big blind.
- A raise must be at least as big as the last bet or raise in that round. Going **all in** for less is always allowed, but it does not let anyone who has already acted raise again, unless the short all-ins together add up to a full raise.
- A bet nobody calls comes back to whoever made it.

## Side pots

When someone is all in, they can only win from each other player as much as they put in themselves. Whatever else is bet goes into a side pot that only the players who put chips in it can win. There can be several side pots.

## The showdown

- After the river, the hands still in are compared: each player's best five of their two cards and the five on the board.
- From the strongest: **royal flush**, **straight flush**, **four of a kind**, **full house**, **flush**, **straight**, **three of a kind**, **two pair**, **pair** and **high card**. An ace can be low in the five-high straight (A-2-3-4-5). The same hand is decided by the cards that make it, then by the kickers.
- Equal hands split the pot; an odd chip goes to the first winner left of the button.
- The last player to bet or raise on the river shows first; after that, only a hand that can win is shown, and a losing hand stays hidden. When everyone is all in, all hands are shown. A player who wins because everyone else folded shows nothing.
- At the end of a hand the winning five cards light up and the hand is named.

## Options

Set by the host in the waiting room.

- **Starting chips**: 1,000, 2,000 or 5,000 each. 1,000 by default.
- **Blinds**: 5 / 10, 10 / 20 or 25 / 50. 10 / 20 by default.
- **Blinds double**: off, every 10 hands or every 20 hands. Every 10 hands by default.
- **Time to act**: off, 20, 30 or 60 seconds. 30 by default. When time runs out you check if you can, or fold, and you are sat out.

## Sitting out, and when someone is away

- **Sit out** when you have to step away: you check or fold whenever it is your turn, and still post your blinds, until you press **I'm back**.
- The host can skip a player the table is waiting on: they check or fold and are sat out, the same as when their time runs out.

## The end

- A player with no chips left is out and watches the rest of the game.
- The game ends when one player has every chip, or after a hand once everyone still playing has pressed **Stop after this hand**. The standings go by chips; those who went out are ranked by who went out last.

## Players

2 to 9 players. A game takes about 45 minutes, less with blinds that double every 10 hands.
