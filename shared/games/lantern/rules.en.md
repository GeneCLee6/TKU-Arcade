## What is Lantern Lane?

A co-operative game played in silence. The palace lanterns along the avenue are numbered 1 to 100, and each of you holds a few. Together you light them all in rising order, without ever saying a number: you only feel when it is your turn. Everyone wins or loses together.

It plays like the card game The Mind (Wolfgang Warsch, NSV); the name, the lanterns, the wording and the look here are our own. Lantern Lane is a working title.

## Please don't talk about numbers

You are probably on a voice call: keep it, the nervous laughter is half the fun. But during a level **don't say numbers, don't count out loud, don't hint** ("I'm way up there", "go, go") and don't give signals. The room's chat is closed until the game ends.

## A level

1. **Deal**: level 1 gives each player 1 lantern, level 2 gives 2, and so on. Lanterns come from a shuffled set of 1 to 100, so many numbers are not in play.
2. **Focus**: look at your lanterns, then press **Hand up**. The level starts the moment everyone's hand is up.
3. **Light**: there are no turns. At any moment, anyone may press the big **Light** button, which lights their lowest lantern onto the avenue. You can only light your lowest.
4. The level is cleared once every lantern is on the avenue. The next level is dealt with one more lantern each.

## Too early

If you light a lantern while someone still holds a lower one, it was too early:

- The table loses a **match**.
- Every lower lantern still in anyone's hand **burns**: it is shown to everyone and goes onto the avenue. Then play goes on.
- When the last match is gone, the night is over and the table loses.

When two players press at almost the same moment, the room takes them in the order they arrived.

## Moonlight

Moonlight helps when the table is stuck. Anyone can press **Moonlight?** to ask for it, and press again to take it back. Once **every** player agrees, one moonlight is used and each player's lowest lantern is shown and goes onto the avenue. That also tells everyone roughly where the others are. You start with 1 moonlight.

## Rewards

Clearing some levels earns the table more:

| Clear level | Reward |
| --- | --- |
| 2 | +1 moonlight |
| 3 | +1 match |
| 5 | +1 moonlight |
| 6 | +1 match |
| 8 | +1 moonlight |
| 9 | +1 match |

The table holds at most 5 matches and 3 moonlight; a reward over that is lost.

## Winning

Clear the last level to win. How many levels depends on the number of players:

| Players | Standard | Short | Taster |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | 12 | 8 | 3 |
| 3 | 10 | 7 | 3 |
| 4 | 8 | 6 | 3 |
| 5 | 7 | 5 | 3 |
| 6 | 6 | 4 | 3 |

The game is meant for 2 to 4; 5 and 6 work, with fewer levels.

## Options

Set by the host in the waiting room.

- **Starting matches (0: one per player)**: 0 to 5. The default 0 gives one match per player (at most 5), the usual difficulty. 1 or 2 is harder, 4 or 5 easier.
- **Levels**: Standard, Short or Taster, from the table above.

## When someone is away

The table waits for every hand. If a player is away, the host can skip them: during the focus their hand goes up; during a level, if everyone else wants moonlight their vote completes it, otherwise their lowest lantern is lit right then (so the host decides when).

## Players

2 to 6 players. A game takes about 20 minutes.
